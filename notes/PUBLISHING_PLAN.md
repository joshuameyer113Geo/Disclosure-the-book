# Publishing Plan: *Disclosure* by Joshua Meyer
*Research done Oct 28, 2026 (PT). Every figure links to its source. Prices change, so check each one again before you pay.*

## TL;DR: recommended route
1. **Start with a proposal sent straight to niche publishers, with self-publishing as the fallback.** Specialist houses take unagented UFO nonfiction: Inner Traditions/Bear & Co, Red Wheel/Weiser (New Page), Llewellyn and Anomalist. Querying them costs nothing.
2. **Query literary agents at the same time** for a Big-5 shot (Kean: Harmony/Crown; Coulthart: HarperCollins). Expect this to be hard without a platform.
3. **If you have no offer after about 6–9 months, self-publish wide.** Use KDP for Amazon plus IngramSpark for stores and libraries, with your own Bowker ISBNs. Add Draft2Digital for ebook library reach and Voices by INaudio or ACX for audio.
4. **Before any of this, handle the GitHub caveat.** Make the repo private, or restrict it to a proposal and outline.

## 0. The GitHub caveat (do this first)
- The full draft is in the public repo `joshuameyer113Geo/Disclosure-the-book`. Publishing an entire work online "typically excludes it from traditional publishing," and editors advise authors not to post a manuscript they plan to submit ([Matesic](https://www.alyssamatesic.com/free-writing-resources/publishing-paths-guide)).
- Some publishers do accept or even run public drafts, such as MIT Press Works in Progress ([MIT Press PubPub](https://mitpressonpubpub.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/pathways/release/1)) and Stripe's Books in Progress ([books.worksinprogress.co](https://books.worksinprogress.co/)). Treat these as exceptions. In nonfiction, the real risks are lost first-serial and exclusivity value and an editor's worry that the content is "already out." An early, rough draft hurts less than a polished one.
- **Actions:**
  - Make the repo private. Git history and forks keep old copies, so the contents may already exist elsewhere.
  - In your query, say that "an early draft was briefly public on GitHub." Don't hide it.
  - Keep future drafting private. Public excerpts should be short blog-length pieces.
  - If you self-publish, the public draft doesn't matter. Note that KDP Select exclusivity requires that the ebook content not be available elsewhere, so take the repo down before enrolling.

## 1. Traditional publishing (via a literary agent)
- **What you need:** Nonfiction sells on a **proposal, not a finished manuscript.** A proposal has these parts:
  - overview/hook
  - author bio and platform (this matters a lot for a first-time UFO author)
  - comparable titles
  - market
  - chapter outline
  - 1–3 sample chapters
- **Agent-side timeline:** About 1–4 weeks to prepare the proposal, 2–4 weeks on submission, 2–3 weeks for publisher replies, then 2–6 months of contract negotiation. The manuscript is due 6–12 months after the deal, followed by about 6–7 months of production ([Josh Bernoff](https://bernoff.com/blog/the-book-pitch-timeline-working-with-agents-and-publishers)). From deal to publication is about 18–24 months ([Wattpad #HowToAuthor](https://www.wattpad.com/416043855-howtoauthor-book-deals-marketing-from-deal-to)).
- **Advances:** For a first-time author, advances are commonly about $5k–$50k ([Bindery Agency](https://www.thebinderyagency.com/articles/what-kind-of-advance-should-i-expect)). They are paid in installments (signing, delivery/acceptance, publication) ([Alia Habib](https://aliahabib.substack.com/p/this-is-delivery-and-acceptance)). Agents take a commission, typically 15% in the US (industry standard).
- **Cost to you:** $0. Legitimate agents never charge reading fees ([Writer Beware/SFWA](https://sfwa.org/other-resources/writer-beware/vanity/)).

## 3. Self-publishing (real costs)
- **ISBNs (Bowker, the only official US agency):** 1 for $125, **10 for $295**, 100 for $575 ([myidentifiers.com](https://www.myidentifiers.com/identify-protect-your-book/isbn/buy-isbn); prices confirmed Aug 5, 2026 by [isbnservices.com](https://www.isbnservices.com/should-you-buy-your-own-isbn/)). Each format needs its own ISBN, so a paperback, hardcover, ebook and audiobook make the 10-pack the right buy ([chapter.pub](https://blog.chapter.pub/isbn-cost/)). Barcodes can be generated free.
- **Amazon KDP:**
  - Setup is free.
  - Ebook royalty is 70% (list price $2.99–$9.99, minus $0.15/MB delivery) or 35% ([KDP pricing](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634500)).
  - Paperback royalty is 60% of list (at $9.99 or more) minus print cost ([KDP paperback royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330)).
- **IngramSpark** (bookstores, libraries, 45,000+ retailers):
  - Title setup is **free**, and revisions have been free since Feb 1, 2026.
  - A **1.875% global distribution fee** applies to sales.
  - You choose the trade discount ([2026 rate card](https://www.ingramspark.com/hubfs/Rate%20Card_IngramSpark_2026.pdf)).
  - Bookstores generally need a 55% discount and returns enabled, which is standard industry practice.
- **Draft2Digital** (ebooks to Apple, Kobo, B&N and libraries such as OverDrive and Hoopla): takes about 10% of retail, and library ebook royalties are paid ([D2D royalty rates](https://draft2digital.com/blog/royalty-rates/), [FAQ](https://draft2digital.com/faq/)).
- **Recommended setup:** KDP for Amazon print and ebook. IngramSpark for print everywhere else (turn off Amazon there). D2D for non-Amazon ebooks, unless you choose KDP Select, which makes the ebook exclusive to Amazon.

## 4. Hybrid publishers and avoiding vanity scams
- **IBPA's 11 criteria:**
  - a defined mission
  - vetting of submissions
  - publishing under its own imprint and ISBNs
  - professional editing and design
  - real distribution
  - a transparent contract
  - royalties higher than standard (generally above 50% of net)
  - respectable sales
  ([IBPA criteria](https://www.ibpa-online.org/page/hybridpublisher)). "Hybrid" is a self-applied label, not a certification.
- **Red flags** ([Writer Beware](https://sfwa.org/other-resources/writer-beware/vanity/)):
  - accepts everything
  - fees with no sales track record
  - pushes upsells (marketing packages, "Hollywood pitches")
  - takes your rights while you pay
  - aggressive cold contact
- For a $0-advance niche book, paying a hybrid $5k–$15k+ rarely beats self-publishing, where you hire the same services yourself and keep the rights.

## 5. Audiobook
- **ACX (Audible):**
  - Must be **human-narrated**; "unauthorized use of text-to-speech, AI" is prohibited (ACX requirements of Apr 2026, as quoted by [audiobookgenerator.ai](https://audiobookgenerator.ai/blog/does-acx-accept-ai-audiobooks)).
  - Narrators who join the invite-only Voice Replica beta can use AI replicas of their own voices ([ACX blog](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/now-in-beta-narrator-voice-replicas-on-acx)).
  - Payment is **per finished hour (PFH), $250–$400** for experienced narrators; SAG-AFTRA's minimum is about $250 PFH. A 10-hour book costs about **$2,500–$4,000**. The alternative is Royalty Share (a 50/50 split for 7 years, $0 upfront) ([midsummerr](https://www.midsummerr.com/blog/audiobook-production-services-compared); example rate card at $300 PFH: [paigevoice](https://www.paigevoice.com/for-independent-authors)).
  - Royalties are 50% exclusive or 30% non-exclusive (per [audiobookgenerator.ai](https://audiobookgenerator.ai/blog/does-acx-accept-ai-audiobooks)).
- **KDP Virtual Voice (AI):** Invite-only and US-only. Free during the beta, pays a 40% royalty, prices are capped at $3.99–$14.99, and titles are labeled as AI-narrated (same source).
- **Voices by INaudio (formerly Findaway Voices by Spotify):** Free, non-exclusive, and you keep 80% of what they receive. Distributes wide, including to libraries ([INaudio support](https://voicessupport.inaudio.com/en/articles/3223552)).
- An 80–100k-word nonfiction book runs roughly 9–11 finished hours, at about 9,300 words per hour (industry rule of thumb).

## 6. Editing, cover, legal
- **Editing (EFA 2026 median rates, nonfiction)** ([EFA](https://www.the-efa.org/rates/), [2026 chart](https://www.the-efa.org/2026-editorial-rate-chart/)):
  - developmental: 4.0–5.0¢ per word, so **$3,200–$4,000 at 80k words**
  - copyediting: 3.0–4.0¢ per word, so **$2,400–$3,200**
  - Proofreading is extra.
- **Cover:** Most covers cost $350–$1,200; the median is about $930 ([Bookstrapper](https://bookstrapper.com/guides/book-cover-design-cost/), [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/book-cover-design-cost/)).
- **Defamation and privacy legal read:** This matters here. The book names real witnesses, officials, researchers and whistleblowers, and makes claims about cover-ups.
  - A US prepublication vet costs about **$1,750–$4,000+** ([Perry Law](https://www.josephperrylaw.com/legal-fees)).
  - A full clearance with a letter costs **$5,000–$9,500** ([Muller Media Law](https://www.mullermedialaw.com/guides/libel-read-cost/)).
  - Traditional publishers usually do this vetting themselves, but contracts include author warranties and indemnities.
  - **Practices to follow:**
    - Source every factual claim to documents (FOIA, congressional record, published interviews).
    - Label opinion as opinion.
    - Avoid private facts about private individuals.
    - Be careful with living, non-public figures.
- **Images and fair use:**
  - US government works (e.g., Navy UAP videos, official reports) are generally public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105.
  - News photos and other authors' images need a license, or a fair-use analysis for transformative commentary. Fair use is a defense, not a permission.
  - Budget **$0–$1,500** for image licenses, or rely on public-domain government material. This is a planning estimate, not a cited figure.
- **Copyright registration:**
  - Currently **$45** (Single Application) or **$65** (Standard) ([copyright.gov fees](https://copyright.gov/about/fees.html)).
  - A proposed increase to $55/$85 could take effect after Nov 2026 ([Fee Study 2026](https://www.copyright.gov/rulemaking/feestudy2026/)).
  - Register within 3 months of publication to preserve statutory damages ([Bowker summary](https://www.myidentifiers.com/self-publishing/self-publishing-discount-packages)).

## 8. Step-by-step timeline
1. **Week 1:** Make the GitHub repo private. Decide the positioning: a sociology of ufology (comps: Pasulka's *American Cosmic*, Kean, Coulthart, Thompson's *UFO Paradox*).
2. **Months 1–3:** Write the proposal and polish 2–3 sample chapters. Build platform with a Substack or newsletter of short excerpts (not the full draft).
3. **Month 3:** Query 20–40 nonfiction agents who handle history, current affairs or popular science. At the same time, submit to Anomalist, Inner Traditions, Red Wheel/Weiser (New Page) and Llewellyn, plus 2–3 university presses.
4. **Months 3–9:** Wait. Inner Traditions' rule is no reply in 8 weeks means no.
   - **If you get an offer:** sign (with an agent or a contract attorney). Publication follows in about 18–24 months.
   - **If not:** go to step 5.
5. **Months 9–15:** Finish the manuscript, then developmental edit, then revise, then copyedit, then legal read, then proofread. Buy the ISBN 10-pack. Commission the cover.
6. **Months 15–16:** Format the files. Upload to KDP and IngramSpark (and D2D). Set up a preorder. Send ARCs to reviewers and podcasts in the UFO and UAP community.
7. **Launch, then about +3 months:** Register the copyright within 3 months. Produce the audiobook (ACX or INaudio). Pitch libraries through Ingram and OverDrive.
