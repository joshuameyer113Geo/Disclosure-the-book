# Ancient Timeline: The Deep Roots of UFO Lore

A sociological history. For each entry: **Source** = what the primary material actually says; **Interpretation** = when the UFO/ancient-astronaut reading appeared and who made it; **Scholarship** = the mainstream academic view. The main point running through it: nearly every "ancient UFO" reading dates to **1947 or later**. It arose after Kenneth Arnold's "flying saucers" and the Space Age, and was read back onto older material.

---

## Prehistoric (c. 40,000 to 3000 BCE): Rock art "astronauts"
- **Source:** Petroglyphs and pictographs of anthropomorphs with radiating heads or "helmets." The examples cited most often are Val Camonica, Italy (Camunian rock art spanning millennia); the Wandjina paintings of the Kimberley, Australia (rain and creation spirits in living Aboriginal tradition, drawn with haloed heads and no mouths); and Barrier Canyon-style figures in Utah (e.g., Sego Canyon).
- **Interpretation:** These images were popularized as "spacemen" in 1960s–70s ancient-astronaut books. Erich von Däniken, *Chariots of the Gods?* (1968), was the most prominent of them, and the Wandjina appear in that literature.
- **Scholarship:** Archaeologists and Aboriginal custodians read these figures as ritual, shamanic, or ancestral beings. The haloes are headdresses or the Wandjina's rain and cloud attributes. The space reading has no support in context, and Aboriginal communities regard it as appropriation.
- **Citations:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandjina ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariots_of_the_Gods%3F

## c. 2144–2124 BCE onward: The Anunnaki in cuneiform
- **Source:** The term *Anunna/Anunnaki* first appears in inscriptions of Gudea of Lagash and of the Ur III dynasty. It means roughly "offspring of An" (the sky god), a collective name for the senior gods. Its meaning shifts over time:
  - Some texts give it as the "seven gods who decree."
  - One text links fifty Anunnaki to Eridu.
  - In *Inanna's Descent* they are seven underworld judges.
  - In the standard Akkadian *Gilgamesh* (c. 1200 BCE) they are underworld judges who "set the land aflame."
- **What the source does not say:** No cuneiform text calls the Anunnaki visitors from another planet. No text names a planet "Nibiru" in Sitchin's sense; *Nibiru* is an astronomical term, usually tied to Marduk and Jupiter. No text describes them mining gold on Earth.
- **Interpretation:** Zecharia Sitchin, *The Twelfth Planet* (1976), and his later *Earth Chronicles* books. Sitchin cast the Anunnaki as Nibiru-dwellers who came to Earth for gold. The idea was later amplified by TV (e.g., *Ancient Aliens*, 2009–).
- **Scholarship:** Assyriologists reject Sitchin's translations and etymologies. Michael S. Heiser compiled a point-by-point critique of them at sitchiniswrong.com.
- **Citations:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anunnaki ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zecharia_Sitchin ; https://www.sitchiniswrong.com/

## c. 2000–1700 BCE: Sumerian creation of humans (Enki and Ninmah); Enki and Ninhursag
- **Source:** In Sumerian myth, the gods grow tired of labor, and Enki and the mother goddess fashion humans from clay to do the work. *Enki and Ninhursag* is set in Dilmun, a pure land without sickness. It includes a forbidden-plants episode in which Enki eats eight plants and is cursed. A rib-related wordplay (Nin-ti, "lady of the rib" / "lady of life") is often compared to Eve.
- **Interpretation:** Sitchin read the creation of humans as genetic engineering of hominids by the Anunnaki. This is the source of the "Adam as hybrid" lore.
- **Scholarship:** Scholars treat these as myths explaining why humans exist: to serve the gods. The "rib" pun linking Nin-ti to Eve was proposed by S. N. Kramer and is debated. Genetic readings have no textual basis.
- **Citations:** ETCSL, Enki and Ninhursag, https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/section1/tr111.htm ; Enki and Ninmah, https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/section1/tr112.htm

## c. 1600 BCE (tablet from Nippur): The Eridu Genesis / Sumerian Flood Story
- **Source:** A fragmentary Sumerian tablet tells of:
  - the creation of humans and animals;
  - the first cities and kingship "lowered from heaven";
  - a flood sent by the gods' decision;
  - Ziusudra, warned by Enki, surviving in a boat and being granted eternal life in Dilmun.
- **Interpretation:** Ancient-astronaut writers read kingship "descending from heaven" as literal arrival from space (Sitchin and others, 1970s onward).
- **Scholarship:** The tablet is a precursor of the Atrahasis, Gilgamesh XI, and Genesis 6–9 flood traditions. Thorkild Jacobsen coined the name "Eridu Genesis" (1981). "Descent from heaven" is the standard divine-legitimation formula for kingship, also found in the Sumerian King List.
- **Citations:** https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/section1/tr174.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eridu_Genesis

## c. 1700 BCE (Old Babylonian): Atrahasis
- **Source:** In this Akkadian epic, the junior Igigi gods rebel against hard labor. Humans are made from clay mixed with the flesh and blood of a slain god (We-ila). Plague, drought, and flood follow, and Atrahasis survives.
- **Interpretation:** This is the core text behind Sitchin's "slave race engineered by gods" thesis.
- **Scholarship:** Read as a labor and theodicy myth. The Lambert & Millard edition (1969) is standard.
- **Citation:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atra-Hasis

## 14th century BCE (Amarna copy) and later: Adapa
- **Source:** Adapa, a sage and priest of Ea at Eridu, breaks the wing of the South Wind and is summoned to Anu's heaven. Ea has warned him to refuse the "bread and water of death." Anu actually offers the bread and water of *life*, so Adapa forfeits immortality. Fragments survive from Amarna in Egypt and from Ashurbanipal's library.
- **Interpretation:** Ancient-astronaut authors read the "ascent to heaven" as a space journey. The story is also widely compared to Adam, sometimes with a claimed Adapa/Adam name link.
- **Scholarship:** Scholars do compare Adapa with Genesis 3: lost immortality through food and a divine test. The Adam=Adapa etymology is not accepted. Adapa is linked to the apkallu, the seven antediluvian sages.
- **Citation:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adapa

## c. 10th–5th century BCE (composition debated): Genesis 1–6, Adam and Eve, the Nephilim
- **Source:** Genesis 2–3 tells of Eden, the tree of knowledge, the serpent, and expulsion. Genesis 6:1–4 has the "sons of God" taking human wives, with the Nephilim on the earth. The later *1 Enoch* (Book of the Watchers, c. 3rd century BCE) expands this into 200 descending Watchers who teach forbidden arts.
- **Interpretation:** Von Däniken (1968) and Sitchin (1976) read the "sons of God"/Nephilim as extraterrestrials interbreeding with humans. This is the root of modern "alien bloodline" lore. Later, Christian UFO-demonology writers recast Watchers as aliens.
- **Scholarship:** Biblical scholars see Genesis 1–11 drawing on and responding to Mesopotamian traditions: flood, creation from clay, the Adapa-like loss of immortality. Genesis 6 is read as a fragment of divine-council and giant myth.
- **Citations:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephilim ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Enoch

## c. 1290–1213 BCE: Abydos "helicopter" (Temple of Seti I)
- **Source:** These are hieroglyphs on a lintel in Seti I's temple at Abydos. Seti's titulary was plastered over and recut with Ramesses II's titulary. When the plaster fell away, the overlapping signs formed shapes that look like a helicopter and aircraft.
- **Interpretation:** Photos circulated in fringe and early-internet circles from the late 1990s. They were later featured on TV.
- **Scholarship:** Egyptologists identify it as a palimpsest: recarved text, with the "helicopter" made of overlapping titles. Both inscriptions can be read.
- **Citation:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abydos_helicopter

## Claimed reign of Thutmose III (1479–1425 BCE); actually surfaced in 1953: the "Tulli Papyrus"
- **Source:** No papyrus has ever been produced. What exists is a 1953 "translation" sent by Italian Fortean Boris de Rachewiltz to Tiffany Thayer. Thayer published it in *Doubt* (Fortean Society), no. 43, as "Forteana ca 1500 BC." It describes "circles of fire" in the sky in Year 22.
- **Interpretation:** UFO authors adopted it immediately in 1953 and through the 1950s–60s.
- **Scholarship and provenance:**
  - Rachewiltz later admitted he never saw the papyrus. He said he worked from notes of Alberto Tulli, the Vatican Museums' Egyptology curator, made in 1934 in Cairo.
  - Rachewiltz also said Thayer falsified his account.
  - Tulli's successor, Gianfranco Nolli, suspected a fake.
  - Samuel Rosenberg, writing in the 1968 Condon Report, judged it plagiarized from Ezekiel. He used it as his example of unverified UFO "history."
- **Citation:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulli_Papyrus

## c. 593 BCE (setting): Ezekiel's vision (Ezekiel 1)
- **Source:** Ezekiel describes a storm wind, fire, four living creatures, "wheels within wheels," and a throne above a firmament.
- **Interpretation:** Von Däniken (1968), then NASA engineer Josef Blumrich, *The Spaceships of Ezekiel* (1973). Blumrich set out to debunk the idea and came away a convert.
- **Scholarship:** Scholars read it as throne-chariot (merkabah) theophany imagery drawn from ancient Near Eastern iconography of cherubim and divine thrones.
- **Citation:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_F._Blumrich

## 218 BCE onward: Roman "prodigies"
- **Source:** Livy (21.62) records "phantom ships" (*navium speciem*) shining in the sky in 218 BCE. Julius Obsequens' *Book of Prodigies* (4th century CE) catalogs celestial omens.
- **Interpretation:** These were collected as "ancient UFOs" by Charles Fort's successors and by Jacques Vallée & Chris Aubeck, *Wonders in the Sky* (2009).
- **Scholarship:** Romans treated such sightings as religious omens to be expiated. They are probably meteors, auroras, or parhelia, filtered through the political reporting of prodigies.
- **Citation:** https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Livy/21*.html (Livy 21.62)

## Interpretive layer, 1919–1976: Who invented "ancient aliens"
- 1919: Charles Fort, *Book of the Damned*, proposes sky visitors in old records.
- 1953: the Tulli Papyrus appears via Fort's *Doubt*.
- 1959: Soviet ethnologist Matest Agrest suggests that Sodom's destruction and Baalbek reflect alien visitors. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matest_M._Agrest
- 1960: Pauwels & Bergier, *Le Matin des magiciens*. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Morning_of_the_Magicians
- 1962: Paul Misraki (as "Paul Thomas"), *Les Extraterrestres*, reads biblical events as UFOs. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Misraki
- 1968: von Däniken, *Chariots of the Gods?*, a mass-market breakthrough.
- 1969: Jacques Vallée, *Passport to Magonia*, flips the argument: UFO reports continue fairy and folklore patterns. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Vall%C3%A9e
- 1976: Robert Temple, *The Sirius Mystery* (Dogon). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sirius_Mystery ; Sitchin, *The Twelfth Planet*.
- Overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_astronauts

## Gaps (where the record goes quiet)
1. **No primary text anywhere describes an extraterrestrial craft or a being from another planet.** Every UFO reading is a post-1947 overlay on religious or omen language.
2. **The Anunnaki gap:** There are no cuneiform references to Nibiru as an inhabited planet, to gold mining, or to genetic engineering. The leap from "offspring of An" to "astronauts" comes from Sitchin, not from any tablet.
3. **The Tulli Papyrus is missing.** No physical object, no museum inventory number, and no photograph has ever been produced. The chain runs only through 1953 correspondence.
4. **Prehistory has no texts.** Rock art carries no inscriptions, so any meaning comes from ethnography or projection. Where living traditions exist (Wandjina), they contradict the alien reading.
5. **Adam and Eve have no single Mesopotamian source.** The parallels (Adapa, Enki and Ninhursag, Atrahasis) are motif-level. No tablet tells the Eden story itself.
6. **Composition dates are uncertain:** Genesis (J/P debate), Eridu Genesis (only one fragmentary tablet), and Adapa (fragments).
7. **c. 400 CE to the medieval period:** Sky omens continue in chronicles, but systematic "ancient UFO" lore thins out until the 1561 Nuremberg and 1566 Basel broadsheets. Those fall outside this ancient scope.
8. **The Egyptian record:** Beyond the palimpsest and the Tulli fabrication, no genuine Egyptian text has ever been seriously put forward as a sighting.
