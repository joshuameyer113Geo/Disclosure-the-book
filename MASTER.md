## Project stance

*Disclosure* is a documentary history of the UFO phenomenon. We write it as unbiased documenters who ask questions and do not hand down verdicts. Per rules/STORY_RULES.md: real people keep their real names; real reported events and established UFO lore are canon and are presented as reported, with dates and sources; nothing is invented; creative filling-in is allowed only when grounded in researched, cited context; and research comes first, so drafting waits on the historian's dated timeline.

### Chapter 9: Inventing Ancient Aliens

*Old Stories, New Witnesses*

Status: draft. Footnotes cite permanent Historian IDs (AO- from history/ARYAN_OCCULT.md, MX- from history/MYTH_CROSSREF.md). Footnotes marked **[unverified, no ID yet]** use live web sources that Historian has not yet assigned an ID.

#### Introduction

This chapter puts two bodies of material side by side. One is old: myths, chronicles, and folk accounts of beings who arrive from the sky or the sea, teach or threaten, and leave. The other is recent: reports from pilots, police officers, and ordinary people who say they saw something they could not identify. Some of them say they met someone.

I am not trying to prove that the old stories describe the same thing the modern witnesses report. I want to know what actually repeats and what does not, and where each comparison came from. Most "ancient UFO" parallels were not found in the old texts. Modern writers brought them there, usually after 1947 and usually for readers already primed by flying-saucer news. So each section below asks two questions: what does the primary source say, and who first drew the UFO parallel, and when? Sometimes the second answer is unknown. When it is, I say so rather than guess.

The chapter closes with two harder topics. The first is the cases that fit no pattern. The second is the explanation that skeptics and witnesses both treat with care: that all of this is mental illness.

#### The interpreters came first

The habit of reading old records as visitations has a traceable start. In 1919, Charles Fort's *The Book of the Damned* proposed that visitors from the sky could be found in neglected records.[^MX-013] W. Raymond Drake, a British follower of Fort, then published *Gods or Spacemen?* in 1964, four years before Erich von Däniken's *Chariots of the Gods?* (1968).[^T-014] Drake went on to issue regional volumes, among them *Gods and Spacemen in the Ancient East* (1968), *Gods and Spacemen in the Ancient West* (1974), and *Gods and Spacemen in Greece and Rome* (1976).[^MX-014] Those books stand behind many of the parallels below, though their contents have not yet been read page by page for this chapter.

#### Norse lore: Thor and Odin

**Primary sources.** Thor, the hammer Mjöllnir, and Odin, who wanders and hung on the world-tree for knowledge, come down to us mainly through two Icelandic works. One is the Poetic Edda, preserved in the thirteenth-century Codex Regius. The other is the Prose Edda that Snorri Sturluson compiled around 1220.[^MX-001] Snorri was a Christian writer, and he rationalized the gods as human kings from Troy whom later people worshipped. Colavito points out that the Greeks and the Norse both "rationalized their gods as allegedly real ancient human kings."[^MX-015] That medieval move is the earliest ancestor of the modern ancient-astronaut argument: the gods were really people, only more advanced.

**Who drew the UFO parallel.** Modern ancient-astronaut media present Thor's hammer and Odin's travels as remembered technology. That much is a pseudoscientific reinterpretation, not a scholarly reading.[^MX-002] The Historian found no author who explicitly connects the Norse gods to the modern "Nordic" alien type.[^MX-004] A shared word is not a shared lineage, and this chapter does not treat it as one. **Open question:** who first connected Thor or Odin to UFOs in print, and when?

#### Greek gods

**Primary sources.** Hesiod's *Theogony* (c. 700 BCE) and the Homeric poems describe the Olympians: gods who quarrel, take human lovers, father half-divine children, and come down to intervene in human affairs. Prometheus brings fire to humanity and is punished for it.

**Who drew the UFO parallel.** Drake's bibliography includes a volume titled *Gods and Spacemen in Greece and Rome* (1976).[^MX-014] Its contents have not been read for this chapter, so the title suggests, but does not confirm, that the parallel was in print by the mid-1970s. Nor would it show that he was the first, and the Historian found no source for a named alien reading of Prometheus or of the Olympians in particular.[^MX-007] **Open question:** the earliest specific alien reading of Prometheus or the Olympians.

#### Chinese dragons

**Primary sources.** Sima Qian's *Records of the Grand Historian* (*Shiji*, 109–91 BCE) describes the culture hero Fu Xi as having "a serpent's body, a man's head." It says that "there being a dragon omen, he enrolled dragons among his officers," and that he drew the eight trigrams.[^MX-015] In Chinese tradition the dragon is a creature of water, weather, and imperial authority. It is not a vehicle.[^MX-008]

**Who drew the UFO parallel.** Colavito traces a 2012 *Ancient Aliens* claim, that ancient Chinese kings received texts from beings "down from the sky," to the Fu Xi material. He finds no such sky-visitor story in the early sources. In those texts the early rulers act on earth, with only symbolic references to Heaven.[^MX-015] The best-known "Chinese alien" artifact, the Dropa stones, traces back no further than July 1962. The story first appeared in the German magazine *Das Vegetarische Universum*, under the name "Reinhardt Wegemann," a writer no one has been able to trace. No record of the 1937 expedition, the scholar said to have translated the discs, or the discs themselves has been found. Later retellings added details: David Agamon's *Sungods in Exile* (1978) added a Dropa people from Sirius, and its author later called that book a hoax.[^MX-022] Who wrote the 1962 article, and why, remains unknown. **Open question:** the first printed reading of Chinese dragons as craft. Drake's 1968 volume on the "Ancient East" is a likely candidate, but nobody has checked it page by page. The earliest dated dragons-as-craft claim Historian has logged is Hartwig Hausdorf's *The Chinese Roswell* (1998); whether anyone made it earlier remains open.[^MX-009]

#### Shore arrivals: the Utsuro-bune and Xu Fu

**Utsuro-bune (1803).** Several Edo-period texts record that a round vessel drifted ashore in Hitachi Province (now Ibaraki Prefecture) in 1803. Inside was a young woman in unfamiliar clothes who could not speak Japanese and would not let anyone near the box she carried. The fishermen pushed her back out to sea. The texts include Komai Norimura's *Ōshuku zakki* (c. 1815), *Toen shōsetsu* (1825, compiled by the literary circle of Kyokutei Bakin), *Hyōryū kishū* (1835), and *Ume no chiri* (1844).[^MX-010] Kazuo Tanaka, a professor emeritus at Gifu University, has studied the surviving records, which now number 14 copies in 13 types per Kamisu (2024).[^MX-017] He reads the episode as folklore and literary embellishment. In none of the accounts does the vessel fly or move under its own power.[^MX-010] An exhibition catalogue from the city of Kuki notes that the Edo period had no concept of an unidentified flying object. The vessel was treated as a foreign ship. Only after the postwar saucer reports, from the Shōwa 30s (1955–64) onward, did Japanese magazines begin calling it an Edo-period UFO encounter; the catalogue names no writer or magazine.[^MX-016] **Open question:** the Historian found no source naming who first read the Utsuro-bune as a UFO. The decade can be dated. The author cannot.

**Xu Fu (219 BCE).** The *Shiji* records that Xu Fu, an alchemist from Qi, asked the First Emperor of Qin for permission to sail in search of Penglai and the other islands of the immortals. The emperor sent him with several thousand boys and girls.[^MX-011] The *Shiji* does not name Japan. The identification of his landfall with Japan appears in later works such as the monk Yichu's *Yichu liutie*, compiled 945–954 and printed in 973; its Japan/Xu Fu passage dates from after 958.[^MX-018] This story runs the other way from the Utsuro-bune: a mainland expedition becomes, in later tradition, the founding of a people across the sea. **Open question:** no source was found for anyone reading Xu Fu as a UFO or alien episode. He belongs in this chapter as a comparison case for arrival-from-the-sea stories, not as part of the UFO record.

#### The Nazi-era search for "Aryan" origins

This material belongs in the chapter because later UFO lore borrowed from it heavily. The historical record and the legend need to be kept apart.

**Thule Society.** It was founded in Munich in August 1918 by Rudolf von Sebottendorff (born Rudolf Glauer) as a cover name for the Bavarian lodge of the Germanenorden, a völkisch, antisemitic order founded in 1912.[^AO-002][^AO-024] Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke's *The Occult Roots of Nazism* remains the standard scholarly account.[^AO-003]

**Ahnenerbe.** The SS Ahnenerbe was founded on July 1, 1935, at the urging of Heinrich Himmler and Herman Wirth. According to Germany's Federal Archives, its purpose was to supply "scientific" proof of "Aryan" superiority. It also housed a research post defending Hanns Hörbiger's World Ice Theory.[^AO-004][^AO-005] Its 1938–39 Tibet expedition was led by the zoologist Ernst Schäfer. The anthropologist Bruno Beger measured Tibetans' skulls and faces to study the "Nordic race in this region."[^AO-006] Isrun Engelhardt's research shows that Himmler pushed for esoteric aims while Schäfer planned a scientific survey and refused to take the World Ice theorist Edmund Kiss along.[^AO-006] The Ahnenerbe was later involved in human experiments and the assembly of a skeleton collection.[^AO-004] That history should not be romanticized.

**Vril.** "Vril" comes from fiction: Edward Bulwer-Lytton's novel *The Coming Race* (1871).[^AO-001] In 1947 Willy Ley described a small Berlin group that searched for Vril, and he wrote that "they knew that the book was fiction."[^AO-008] The "Vril Society" as a powerful Nazi lodge was first claimed by Louis Pauwels and Jacques Bergier in *Le Matin des magiciens* (1960).[^AO-013] In 1992, Norbert Jürgen-Ratthofer and Ralf Ettl merged it with the postwar myth of Nazi flying discs.[^AO-014]

**Schwabenland.** The German Antarctic expedition of 1938–39, aboard the ship *Schwabenland* and led by Alfred Ritscher, surveyed the region it called Neuschwabenland, in present-day Dronning Maud Land. It did not build a base.[^AO-007] In 1974 Ernst Zündel, writing as Christof Friedrich, published *UFOs: Nazi Secret Weapon?*, which claimed that saucers flew from a secret Antarctic base.[^AO-015] Colin Summerhayes and Peter Beeching examined the claim and found no evidence of any wartime German base.[^AO-025]

**Who drew the UFO parallel.** The documented line runs from Bulwer-Lytton's fiction (1871) to Ley (1947), then to Pauwels and Bergier (1960), Zündel (1974), and Jürgen-Ratthofer and Ettl (1992). The parallel was drawn after the war by writers, and some of them, Zündel among them, were openly sympathetic to Nazism.

#### Dietrich Eckart as legend

Dietrich Eckart was a poet and the publisher of the antisemitic weekly *Auf gut Deutsch*, which received support from the Thule circle. He was a mentor to the young Hitler, who dedicated the second volume of *Mein Kampf* to him. Eckart died in Berchtesgaden on December 26, 1923.[^AO-009][^AO-010][^AO-011] The legend goes further. A supposed deathbed line, "Follow Hitler! He will dance, but it is I who have called the tune," has no confirmed printing earlier than J. H. Brennan's *The Occult Reich* (1974), a book without notes or bibliography. Trevor Ravenscroft's *The Spear of Destiny* (London, 1972) is sometimes named as the source, but it is unconfirmed that the quote appears in it.[^AO-023] Goodrick-Clarke dismissed this whole genre of "Nazi satanism" books.[^AO-012] The quotation has no documented primary source and should not be repeated as fact.

#### Nordics and reptilians: modern lore and its roots

**Nordics.** On Nov 20, 1952, near Desert Center, California, George Adamski said he met Orthon, a human-looking Venusian with long fair hair who warned about nuclear testing.[^T-041] Writers have noted Orthon's resemblance to Klaatu in *The Day the Earth Stood Still* (1951). From 1975, Billy Meier in Switzerland claimed contact with "Pleiadians."[^T-052] The label "Nordic" itself is not datable. The Historian found only that it was common by the 1980s.[^AO-020] **Open question:** the earliest use of "Nordic" for an alien type.

**Reptilians.** David Icke went on BBC *Wogan* on April 29, 1991,[^T-053] and published *The Biggest Secret* in 1999. That book claims shape-shifting reptilian bloodlines rule the world. Critics have shown that its structure reproduces antisemitic conspiracy motifs.[^T-043] In a 2013 poll, 4% of US voters said they believed reptilian people control the world.[^T-044] Ancient serpent figures, from the Eden serpent to the Nāgas, were later read as reptilians. That reading is modern, as Chapter 5 shows. The lineage of reptilian lore before Icke, including pulp fiction, the TV series *V* (1983), and Credo Mutwa, remains an open research item (history/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md).

#### Outliers

Some reports fit none of the recurring types: not grays, not Nordics, not reptilians. They also did not repeat.

**Cat-faced occupants, Brazil (June 1967).** This case is often called the "Itajubá" case, but the earliest account found so far does not put it there. The APRO Bulletin (Nov–Dec 1967) reports that on June 7, 1967, at 1:30 a.m., Geraldo Barqueira (spelled Baqueiro elsewhere) was driving his station wagon up the Serra da Mantiqueira when a blinking light stalled his engine. Under hypnosis he described a round object about 5 m across, with "five faces which resembled those of cats" at its windows. The same Bulletin carries a separate Itajubá report, dated Sept 24, 1967, of three spherical objects and no occupants. How the two became attached is not known. A Brazilian account gives the date as June 5, which is contested. The case reached Jacques Vallée's landing catalogue. In a minute dated May 27, 1971, O. H. Turner of Australia's Joint Intelligence Organisation listed it as Case 848 in a secret paper. That paper is now open at the National Archives of Australia (series A13693, item 30030606).[^MX-019] The Australian file is authentic. The encounter is not verified. It is one witness, recalled under hypnosis, and the account comes through civilian UFO literature. The word "cat-like" records his impression, not an anatomy.

**Quarouble, France (Sept 10, 1954).** Marius Dewilde reported two short, bulky figures in what looked like diving suits near an object on railway tracks, and a light that left him unable to move. The same Australian file lists a Case 144 from France and separately gives a Dewilde account, but the pairing of Case 144 with Quarouble is **contested**: likely, not proven.[^MX-020] Small humanoids later became common, so this case is better described as an early example of a type than as an outlier.

**Research need.** I found no documented source for other non-recurring forms, such as insectoid, robotic, or plant-like beings, that would meet this book's standard. The category stays open for Historian.

#### The mental-illness explanation

One easy explanation for all of this is that the witnesses are ill. The record does not support that explanation in its simple form. It also does not leave the reports unexplained.

**The argument against illness alone.** Visitor stories appear in many cultures and centuries, and modern witnesses include trained observers. Illness alone has trouble accounting for so much agreement. Controlled research has also not found the expected pathology. Nicholas Spanos and colleagues (1993, *Journal of Abnormal Psychology*) compared people who reported UFO experiences with community and student control groups. People with "intense" experiences (contact with beings or missing time) did not score as more psychopathological, less intelligent, or more fantasy-prone than the controls.[^MX-021] Susan Clancy and colleagues at Harvard noted in 2002 that "abductees" seldom show signs of mental illness.[^MX-021]

**The documented counter-explanations.** These same studies point to mechanisms other than illness.

- *Sleep paralysis.* In the Spanos study, intense experiences were more often sleep-related, and intensity correlated with measures of fantasy-proneness and unusual sensory experience.[^MX-021] The folklorist David Hufford's *The Terror That Comes in the Night* (1982) documented Newfoundland's "Old Hag." In that waking paralysis the sleeper feels pressure on the chest and senses a hostile presence. Hufford argued that the core experience is shared across cultures and that each culture names it differently.[^MX-021] Clancy and Richard McNally (2005) found that the accounts of the alleged abductees they studied matched this pattern.[^MX-021] This explanation competes directly with the cross-cultural argument above. A shared feature of human sleep could produce shared descriptions without any shared outside cause.
- *Memory and suggestion.* People who reported recovered memories of abduction were more prone than controls to false recall and recognition in the lab. Hypnotic suggestibility predicted those errors.[^MX-021] Clancy's book *Abducted* (Harvard University Press) describes how a nightmare, a cultural script, and hypnotic regression can combine into a sincere false memory.[^MX-021]
- *Cultural transmission through media.* The best-known test case is the Hills. Their 1961 accounts describe uniformed, broadly human figures. The large wraparound eyes first appear in Barney's hypnosis session of Feb 22, 1964. Martin Kottmeyer argued in 1994 that they echo *The Outer Limits* episode "The Bellero Shield," which aired twelve days earlier. Jason Colavito notes that the resemblance is partial. The link is **disputed**.[^MC-029] Through Fuller's book and the 1975 TV film, the Hills' account then became the template for later abduction narratives.[^T-012]

**Where that leaves the question.** The studies cited here do not find that experiencers are, as a group, mentally ill. They find ordinary mechanisms instead: sleep, memory, suggestion, and shared stories. None of these studies addresses multi-witness or sensor-recorded military cases, which Part II covers. I have not tried to close that gap here.

## Story and canon rules

Source: [rules/STORY_RULES.md](rules/STORY_RULES.md)

1. **Real people keep real names.** No renaming, composites, or pseudonyms for real figures.
2. **Real reported events and established UFO lore are canon.** They are presented as reported, with dates and sources.
3. **Nothing invented.** No fake names, fake places, fake documents, or fabricated events.
4. **Grounded creativity only.** Artistic symbolism and creative filling-in are allowed only when grounded in researched context, with sources cited.
5. **Research first.** Story drafting waits for Eden Historian's dated timeline with imagery attached.

### Footnote convention
- Footnote only sourced claims that need it: a specific description, date, quote, figure, or disputed point (e.g. the gray alien description, pointing to Betty and Barney Hill and the historian's entry). Do not footnote every line or common knowledge.
- Use markdown footnotes keyed to the historian's claim IDs: `[^T-012]` for history/TIMELINE.md entries, `[^MC-005]` for history/MODERN_CASES_FACTS.md entries. Add a short suffix for a second note on the same entry (`[^T-012b]`).
- The footnote text names the entry and its source URLs from the historian file. If no historian entry supports the claim, do not footnote it with an outside source on your own: flag it as a research gap and leave it out of the prose.
- Example (manuscript/part-2-modern-and-military-cases/ch16.md):
  `...became the template for later abduction narratives.[^T-012]`
  `[^T-012]: history/TIMELINE.md [T-012], Betty & Barney Hill (Sept 19-20, 1961). Sources: ...`
- Canon rules above still apply: real names, real reported events and established lore, nothing invented, creative symbolism only when grounded in research.
